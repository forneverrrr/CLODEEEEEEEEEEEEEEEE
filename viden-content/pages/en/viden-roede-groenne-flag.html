Red and green flags — sorted by what they cost

Most checklists are flat: twenty points, all equally important. That does not help when you are holding two properties and have to choose. Here are the same matters sorted by the only thing that actually separates them — what they cost you if you miss them.

First, the repair ladder

Anything that can be found in a property sits on one of six rungs. The rung decides whether a finding is a price argument or a problem.

  • 1. Cosmetics — paint, wallpaper, floors, kitchen doors. A few thousand DKK.
  • 2. Surfaces and fittings — a whole kitchen or bathroom, some windows. 50,000–150,000 DKK.
  • 3. Installations — electrical, heating, drainage, damp. 25,000–180,000 DKK. Electrical in 120 m² typically 65,000–90,000.
  • 4. Structure — roof, foundations, load-bearing elements. Roof on 100 m² typically 50,000–160,000 DKK.
  • 5. Legal matters — easements, association debt, discrepancies in the building register. From zero to unpredictable.
  • 6. Location — depopulation, closing shops and schools. Cannot be repaired with money at all.
What nobody says out loudBuyers react to rung 1 and rarely look closely at rungs 3–6. Sellers know this and typically invest in exactly rung 1 before selling. An ugly kitchen over new wiring is a better deal than a new kitchen over old wiring — but the first one feels worse to walk into.

Class A — can kill the deal

What they have in common: they cannot be reduced to a predictable amount, and they do not get cheaper because you negotiate.

A1

Structural defects not reflected in the price

Roof, foundations or load-bearing elements with a remark, while the price sits at the level of comparable properties without one. The amount is large enough to move the whole transaction, and rarely large enough to be recovered afterwards.

Calculate the amount before you bid, not after.

A2

An easement with financial obligations where the deed text has not been produced

If the listing says «see deed» and the deed is not attached, you genuinely do not know what you are committing to. An easement can contain maintenance obligations on a shared road, connection requirements, or contributions that follow the property rather than the owner.

Ask for the full text. If it cannot be produced, that is the answer in itself.

A3

A housing co-operative with heavy shared debt behind a low share price

A low price on the share can mean the debt sits in the association instead of in the price. It is invisible in the listing but visible in the accounts and in the monthly charge — and a change in the association's loan rate hits your monthly cost directly.

Never compare co-operative prices without holding the monthly charge and the shared loan side by side.

A4

A location in sustained decline

The only item on the whole list you cannot buy your way out of. You can replace the roof, the wiring and the kitchen. You cannot reopen the grocery shop, the school or the bus route. The direction over the past five years says more than today's level.

A5

The seller will not produce a document that ought to be produced

Not because the document necessarily hides anything dramatic, but because a refusal at this stage is the cheapest information you will get in the entire process.

Class B — a concrete price argument

Here the amount can be worked out. It is not a reason to walk — it is a reason to negotiate with a number you can document.

B

The five most common

  • Installations at the end of their life — electrical, heating, windows. Electrical in 120 m²: typically 65,000–90,000 DKK.
  • An oil tank not properly decommissioned — the documentation must exist, otherwise you inherit the task.
  • Upcoming separation of storm and foul drainage — notified by the municipality, paid by whoever owns the property when it happens.
  • Construction damage insurance about to expire — the cover follows the building, not you.
  • A price above the public valuation without explanation — not a defect in itself, but a question that needs an answer.

Trade prices in the Copenhagen area are typically 15–25 % above Jutland levels. Calculate on local prices, not national averages.

Class C — ask, it may turn out to be nothing

C

Inconsistencies, not defects

  • «Newly renovated» with no matching entry in the building register — either the register is not updated, or the work was never notified.
  • Carelessly filled-in fields — «??» in a form means somebody did not know, not that it is fine.
  • Consumption stated «per the seller's estimate» rather than metered.
  • An energy label for the whole building instead of for the apartment.
  • Photographs clearly older than the listing — season, furnishing, weather.

Class C rarely decides anything on its own. Three C's in the same case is a pattern.

Green flags — which almost nobody writes about

Warning lists are easy to write. What is harder and more useful are the signs that a property has actually been looked after.

GREEN

Five things pointing the right way

  • An electrical installation report with no remarks in either category.
  • Actual metered heating consumption rather than an estimate.
  • Regular minutes from the association's general meetings — active accounts are a good sign in themselves.
  • Listing, building register and reports agree — floor area, year of construction, heating.
  • The seller discloses a defect before being asked. That is the strongest single sign on the list.

How to use the three classes when choosing between two properties

Do not count flags. A property with four yellow flags can easily be the better purchase than one with a single red.

  • One red flag without a matching reduction in price: move on to the next property.
  • Orange flags: add the amounts up and subtract them from your ceiling, not from the asking price.
  • Yellow flags: ask the questions in writing before bidding. Answers are free.
  • Green flags: weight them. They are rarer than red ones and say more about how the property has been treated.

In short

A flat checklist tells you what to look for. It does not tell you what it means for your finances — and that is what the choice between two properties actually turns on. Always sort by amount, not by how bad something looks in a photograph.
