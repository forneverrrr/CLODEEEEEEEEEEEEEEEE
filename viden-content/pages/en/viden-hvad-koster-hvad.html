What costs what when you buy a home in Denmark

The purchase price is the number everyone looks at. It is rarely the number that causes the unpleasant surprises. Here is what the rest of the process costs, when each expense arrives, and why almost all of it is priced per property.

What does advice cost?

What matters in this list is not only the price, but when each service becomes relevant and whose interest it serves.

ADVICE
  • Estate agent: 1–4 % of the sale price (typically 1.5–3 %). Paid by the seller, but calculated from the price you pay. Represents the seller's interests by law.
  • Property lawyer: 4,950–15,000 DKK at a fixed price, or 2,000–3,500 DKK per hour. Typically enters around the purchase agreement.
  • Buyer's adviser: 1,795–8,495 DKK per property. Enters once you have chosen the property.
  • Building surveyor: 2,000–6,000 DKK for purchase advice, 3,000–7,000 DKK for a survey.
  • NordIndsigt BoligCheck: from 299 DKK for one property, 599 DKK for three. Enters before you have chosen.

Note the pattern: everything except the last is priced per property and becomes relevant only after you have found the one home you have fallen for.

What do the documents cost?

A condition report (tilstandsrapport) typically costs 2,500–4,000 DKK for an apartment, 3,500–5,500 DKK for a terraced house or smaller villa, and 5,000–8,000 DKK for a villa above 150 m². It is normally ordered by the seller, but it is worth knowing what it costs — and that it is not always available when the property is advertised.

What do the fees cost?

Registration duty (tinglysningsafgift) is a public fee of 0.6 % of the purchase price plus 1,850 DKK (2026). On a 3m DKK home that is 19,850 DKK. It is not negotiable.

What does it cost to change your mind?

You have a right of withdrawal for six working days after the purchase agreement is accepted. Using it costs 1 % of the purchase price. On a 3m DKK home that is 30,000 DKK.

Put the numbers side by sideWithdrawing after the bid: 30,000 DKK. Having the documents read through beforehand: 299 DKK. This is not an argument that one is cheap — it is an argument about when in the process the money is spent.

What do the typical findings cost?

This is why cosmetics are rarely what should worry you most.

ROOF

400–2,500 DKK per m² depending on roof type: roofing felt from about 500 DKK/m², fibre cement 550–950, steel 800–1,300, concrete 1,000–1,400, clay tile 1,200–1,600, thatch 1,400–2,200.

For a 100 m² home: roughly 50,000–160,000 DKK in total.

ELECTRICAL

An electrician's hourly rate is 575–800 DKK. A new consumer unit costs 15,000–35,000 DKK, an HPFI residual current device 3,000–8,000 DKK. A complete electrical update in a 120 m² home typically runs to 65,000–90,000 DKK, with the range for whole houses from 25,000 to 180,000 DKK.

For comparison: painting and surface work costs a few thousand kroner.

Does it cost the same across the country?

No. Trade hourly rates are typically 15–25 % higher in the Copenhagen area than in Jutland. A carpenter costs roughly 500–600 DKK per hour in Copenhagen against 400–500 DKK in West Jutland.

There is a counter-effect that is rarely mentioned, though: in sparsely populated areas travel costs are higher, which offsets part of the saving. So «cheaper in the provinces» is true, but not entirely.

The point

Almost every cost in a property purchase is priced per property and arrives after you have chosen. That is why most buyers never have more than one property professionally compared.

What to calculate before you bid

  • Registration duty: 0.6 % of the purchase price + 1,850 DKK. A known figure — put it in from the start.
  • Advice: which of the above will you genuinely need, and when?
  • Withdrawal: 1 % of the purchase price. What is that in kroner on this particular property?
  • Buffer for findings: could you handle a roof or an electrical update in year two?
  • Geography: is the property somewhere trade hours are at the expensive end?

In short

Do not only ask «what does the property cost?». Ask «what does the whole process cost, and when does each expense land?» Most unpleasant surprises in a property purchase are not the price of the home — they are the costs that sat outside that number.
