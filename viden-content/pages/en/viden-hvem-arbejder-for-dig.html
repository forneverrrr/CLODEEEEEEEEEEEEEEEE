Guides / Who works for you
Guide · Roles

The agent is friendly, helpful and thorough. And he works for the seller

This is not an accusation. It is what the law says. If you are buying in Denmark for the first time, it is probably the single most useful thing to understand before you view anything.

Who represents whom — what the law says

The Danish Act on the sale of real property is clear on this point. The estate agent must look after the seller's needs and interests. And nobody may act as intermediary for both the seller and the buyer in the same transaction.

The agent still has a duty to inform you as a buyer about relevant facts concerning the property. But he is not obliged to advise you, and he is not your adviser.

There is even a detail in the law that few buyers notice: if you do not have an adviser of your own, the agent must tell you that you may seek assistance. The law assumes you have someone on your side — and requires the seller's own agent to remind you of it.

What does the agent earn, and on what?

The agent's commission is calculated as a percentage of the sale price. The rate is typically between 1 and 4 percent, most often around 1.5–3 percent.

  • A home at 1.5m DKK — typically 35,000–55,000 DKK in commission
  • A home at 4m DKK — typically 90,000–130,000 DKK
  • The average sits around 85,000 DKK, but the spread is wide

Two things are worth noting. The commission is negotiable. And none of the large agency chains publish fixed prices — you only get a number when you ask.

Why the agent's arithmetic is not yours

The commission is a percentage of the price you end up paying. That creates a built-in asymmetry worth understanding — not because anyone is acting in bad faith, but because the numbers point in different directions.

THE MATHS

On a home listed at 3,000,000 DKK

  • Agent commission at 2 %: 60,000 DKK
  • You negotiate the price down by 100,000 DKK
  • New commission at 2 % of 2.9m: 58,000 DKK

You save 100,000 DKK. The agent loses 2,000 DKK. The ratio is roughly 50 to 1.

About this calculationThis is an illustration we worked out ourselves to make the mechanics concrete, not an official figure. The exact rate and agreement vary from sale to sale.

It does not mean the agent is working against you. It means he has very little financial reason to fight for a lower price — and that you should therefore not expect it.

What the others cost, and when they arrive

WHO · COST · WHEN · WHOSE INTEREST
  • Estate agent — 1–4 % of the sale price (typically 1.5–3 %) · from day one · the seller's, by law
  • Property lawyer (boligadvokat) — 4,950–15,000 DKK · around the purchase agreement · yours, legally
  • Building surveyor (byggesagkyndig) — 2,000–7,000 DKK · once you are focused on one property · neutral, the building only
  • Buyer's adviser (køberrådgiver) — 1,795–8,495 DKK per property · once you have chosen · yours, but per property
  • NordIndsigt — 299 DKK for one, 599 DKK for three · before you have chosen · yours

Look at the third column. Everyone else becomes relevant only after you have found the one home you have fallen for.

That is not laziness — it is economics. When advice costs from 1,795 DKK per property, having three properties reviewed makes no sense. So nobody does it.

The consequence is that most buyers never have a professional look at a property before they have already decided. The report arrives after the infatuation, and becomes a check on the decision rather than a tool for making it.

What does it cost to change your mind?

You have a right of withdrawal for six working days after the purchase agreement is accepted. Using it costs 1 percent of the purchase price.

On a 3m DKK home that is 30,000 DKK to change your mind. Having the documents read through before you bid costs 299 DKK. This is not an argument that we are cheap — it is an argument about when in the process the money is spent.

A common misunderstandingMany people assume the right of withdrawal works like the 14-day return period from online shops. It does not. It is shorter, it counts in working days, and it is not free.

The point

Everyone in a property transaction has an interest. The question is not whether they are honest — it is what they earn money on, and when they enter the process.

What to ask before you bid

  • Have I been told that I may have my own adviser? The law assumes it — ask if it has not been mentioned.
  • How is the agent's commission calculated? The seller pays it, but it is calculated from the price you pay.
  • Which documents exist, and which have not been produced yet?
  • What would 1 % of this purchase price be, in kroner? That is what changing your mind costs.

Frequently asked questions

Does the estate agent work for the buyer or the seller in Denmark?

For the seller. Danish law on the sale of real property states that the estate agent must look after the seller's needs and interests, and that nobody may act as intermediary for both parties in the same transaction. The agent still has a duty to inform the buyer about relevant facts about the property, but is not the buyer's adviser. The law also requires the agent to tell a buyer without an adviser that they may seek assistance.

What does an estate agent cost in Denmark?

The commission is a percentage of the sale price, typically between 1 and 4 percent and most often around 1.5–3 percent. On a home at 1.5m DKK that is roughly 35,000–55,000 DKK, and on a home at 4m DKK roughly 90,000–130,000 DKK. The average is around 85,000 DKK. The commission is negotiable, and none of the large chains publish fixed prices.

If I negotiate the price down, does the agent lose much?

Very little. On a home listed at 3,000,000 DKK with a 2 percent commission, the agent earns 60,000 DKK. If the price is negotiated down by 100,000 DKK, the commission falls to 58,000 DKK. The buyer saves 100,000 DKK while the agent loses 2,000 DKK — a ratio of roughly 50 to 1. This is an illustration of the mechanics, not an official figure.

What does it cost to withdraw from a property purchase in Denmark?

The right of withdrawal applies for six working days after the purchase agreement is accepted, and using it costs 1 percent of the purchase price. On a home at 3,000,000 DKK that is 30,000 DKK. It is not comparable to the 14-day return period known from online shopping: it is shorter, counts in working days, and is not free.

Who is on the buyer's side before a property has been chosen?

In practice, nobody. Property lawyers, buyer's advisers and building surveyors are all priced per property and become relevant once a buyer has selected one home. Because advice costs from 1,795 DKK per property, comparing several properties professionally is rarely done, which means most buyers only get expert input after they have already decided.

Sources: Act on the sale of real property · agent commission levels · lawyer fees and registration duty. Price levels are typical market levels collected in July 2026 and vary.
