When you should NOT buy the property

Almost every buying guide explains how to buy correctly. Very few write about when to walk away entirely. Here are five situations where the right answer is no — even when the property itself is good, the paperwork is in order and the price is fair.

01

Your ownership horizon is under five years

Transaction costs decide this one. Registration duty alone is 0.6 % of the purchase price plus 1,850 DKK, and on the way out the agent's commission is typically 1–4 % of the sale price. Together, a home often has to rise several percent in value before you break even.

If you expect to move within about five years, calculate whether renting is cheaper before you decide.

02

Your residence permit or job situation is uncertain

A home is one of the most illiquid assets you can own. A sale takes months — in North Jutland a house sale requires on average eight viewings. If you might have to move quickly, you may be forced to sell at a moment you did not choose.

This applies with particular force if you are in Denmark on a permit tied to a specific employer.

03

The town depends on one large employer

Your income and the value of your home then depend on the same factor. If the company closes or cuts back, you can lose your job at the same moment demand for housing in the area falls. The risk hits both sides of your finances at once instead of being spread.

04

Local services are disappearing

Infrastructure is one of the few things about a property that cannot be repaired with money. You can replace the roof, the wiring and the kitchen. You cannot reopen the local shop, the school or the bus route yourself.

A good property in an area with shrinking services is a different kind of risk from a property with technical faults — and a harder one to reverse.

05

The price is at the limit of your income

There is no official rule for how large a buffer to keep, but the order of magnitude can be read from what typical findings cost. A new roof on a 100 m² home runs to 50,000–160,000 DKK, and a comprehensive electrical update to 25,000–180,000 DKK.

Without a buffer for that kind of expense, an ordinary finding in a condition report becomes an emergency instead of a planned cost.

Four questions worth asking yourself

  • What happens if I have to move in two years? Can I sell without being under time pressure?
  • How many employers are within commuting distance? One is too few.
  • Has there been more or less local service in the past five years? The direction matters more than the current level.
  • Do I have 100,000 DKK in reserve after the down payment? If not, what is the plan when the roof needs replacing?

In short

Do not only ask «is there something wrong with this property?». Ask «does this property fit the situation I realistically expect to be in for the next five years?» The first question is answered by the reports. The second you have to ask yourself — and it is the one that costs most if skipped.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I need to stay for buying to make sense in Denmark?

There is no single official figure, but the calculation turns on transaction costs.
