Buy or rent? The arithmetic over 5, 10 and 20 years

The discussion is almost always framed as rent against monthly mortgage payment. That is the wrong comparison, because it misses the one item that actually decides the answer: what it costs to get into a property and out again. Spread over time, that item changes the conclusion completely.

First, what we calculate — and what we do not

We stay with items that have a source. Mortgage interest rates move continuously and we do not have a current figure we are willing to calculate from, so interest is excluded. The same applies to tax and any housing benefit schemes.

What is NOT in the numbers belowInterest and repayments · property value tax and land tax · running ownership costs · ongoing maintenance · price development on the property · differences in insurance. The calculation below shows only the transaction costs — the expense that exists regardless of what interest rates do.

What one round trip costs

Calculated on a property at 3,000,000 DKK:

  • On purchase — registration duty: 0.6 % of the purchase price plus 1,850 DKK = 19,850 DKK
  • On purchase — advice: from 299 DKK upwards. Buyer advisory on the market typically costs 1,795–8,495 DKK.
  • On sale — agent commission: typically 1–4 % of the sale price, most often 1.5–3 % = 45,000–90,000 DKK

In total for one trip in and out again: roughly 65,000–110,000 DKK, equal to about 2–4 % of the property's price. That is the amount the property has to rise by just for you to break even.

The same amount spread across the holding period

This is where the difference becomes visible. The amount is identical — only the number of years changes.

5 YEARS

13,000–22,000 DKK a year

Roughly 1,100–1,800 DKK a month in pure transaction cost, before you have paid a single krone in interest, tax or maintenance. On that horizon the property has to rise noticeably just for buying not to cost more than renting.

Below about five years, calculate renting properly before you decide.

10 YEARS

6,500–11,000 DKK a year

Around 540–920 DKK a month. The item has halved by changing one thing: how long you stay. Meanwhile repayments have had time to build equity.

20 YEARS

3,250–5,500 DKK a year

Under 460 DKK a month. On that horizon transaction costs have essentially stopped mattering, and the comparison is decided instead by interest, maintenance and price development.

For comparison: what renting costs

Official figures for 2026: social housing (almene familieboliger) averages 960 DKK per m² a year, private rental 1,591 DKK per m² a year. Regionally the level ranges from about 791 to 1,285 DKK per m² a year, highest in the Copenhagen area and on Zealand, lowest in West and South Jutland.

For a 100 m² home that is roughly 96,000 DKK a year in social housing and roughly 159,000 DKK a year in private rental.

National averages hide very large local differences. Use the figure as an order of magnitude, not as the price of a specific home.

The item people forget entirely

Maintenance on an owned home does not arrive evenly. It arrives in single amounts:

  • New roof, about 100 m²: typically 50,000–160,000 DKK
  • Comprehensive electrical update: typically 25,000–180,000 DKK — 120 m² typically lands at 65,000–90,000 DKK
  • A whole kitchen or bathroom: typically 50,000–150,000 DKK

In a rented home that type of expense is carried by the landlord. In an owned home it is carried by you, at a moment you do not choose. That is not an argument against owning — it is an argument for holding a buffer, and for knowing what is coming before you sign.

How to calculate your own situation

  • How long will you realistically stay? Not how long you hope — how long you expect. That is the variable that weighs most.
  • Multiply the property price by 2–4 %. That is your round-trip transaction cost. Divide by the number of years.
  • Add what is outstanding on installations and roof. It is in the reports, and it is the amount most often missing from people's arithmetic.
  • Then compare with annual rent for a similar property in the same area — not with the national average.

In short

The question is rarely «rent or mortgage payment». It is «how many years do I have to spread the costs over». On a short horizon renting wins almost regardless of interest rates. On a long horizon transaction costs become small, and the choice turns on entirely different things — the area, the condition of the property, and what is waiting on rungs 3 and 4.

Sources: registration duty · agent commission · Landsbyggefonden rent statistics 2026 · BL facts and figures on rent · roof prices · electrical prices. The calculations are examples based on a 3,000,000 DKK, 100 m² property and are not a valuation of any specific home. Price levels collected July 2026.
